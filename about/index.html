About Student Recourse Canada

Student Recourse Canada is a private advocacy and dispute-resolution service for students in Canada who were failed by a private career college.

When a college misleads a student — about job placement, accreditation, refunds, fees, or a program that was cancelled — ways to complain do exist. They are just scattered across provincial regulators, each with its own process and its own deadline, and the deadlines are short. In Alberta a student has six months from their last day of attendance. Most find that out after it has run out.

We help students document what happened, meet the deadline that applies to them, and put a properly prepared complaint in front of the right decision-maker.

What we do

  • Work out which regulator applies and when the deadline falls, with the government source cited for every date
  • Organise the account, the timeline and the evidence to a standard a regulator or a court can actually use
  • Preserve evidence — files are cryptographically hashed when they arrive, and marketing pages are archived with a timestamp before the college changes them
  • Prepare complaint documents from the student's own information, for the student to read, approve, sign and file

What we do not do

Student Recourse Canada is not a law firm. We do not give legal advice, we do not represent anyone before a court or tribunal, and we do not act as anyone's agent in a legal proceeding. The student is always the complainant and the student always decides. Where a matter needs a lawyer, we say so and refer it out.

We also make no claims about outcomes. Nobody can promise what a regulator will decide, and anyone who does is selling something.

Who runs it

My name is Robin Lauscher. I am a software developer in Alberta, and between 2022 and 2024 I went through my own dispute with a private career college here.

The hard part was not arguing the case. It was working out what the process actually was, and finding out how little time was left to use it. That is why this starts with a deadline checker rather than a sales page.

The platform behind the service — the deadline engine, the evidence vault and the document generator — was built in-house rather than bought, so every date it produces can be traced back to the regulation it came from.

Where we work

Alberta first, because that is where the shortest and least forgiving deadline sits. British Columbia and Ontario follow.

What is available today

The deadline checker is free, public, and open to anyone. It asks three questions, shows the deadlines that apply, and links each one to the government page it comes from. It collects nothing — there is no account, no tracking, and nothing you type leaves your browser.

Case work opens in January 2027. Alberta's Private Vocational Training Amendment Act comes into force on 1 January 2027, bringing new student rights and a tuition protection fund. We are not taking case files before then.

If you work with students

Settlement agencies, student legal clinics, students' associations and community legal workers meet these students long before we do — often after the window has already closed. The deadline checker is free to use and free to hand on, and there is nothing to sign up for.
