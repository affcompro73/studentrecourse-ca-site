What to do next

  1. Save everything todayYour enrolment contract, receipts, emails, and screenshots of the pages that made the promises. Colleges edit their websites once a dispute starts. A screenshot with a date is evidence; a memory is not.
  2. Complain to the college in writingEvery regulator expects you to use the college's own complaint process first, and to be able to prove you did. Ask them for their written complaint procedure — they are required to give it to you.
  3. File with the regulator before your window closesIn Alberta that is the Private Career Colleges and Compliance Branch, at pcc.compliance@gov.ab.ca. Filing a thin complaint on time beats a perfect one filed late.
Alberta's rules are changing. The Private Vocational Training Amendment Act received Royal Assent in November 2025, and most of it comes into force on 1 January 2027. It creates a student tuition protection fund and new obligations for colleges. The six-month complaint window is unchanged as of 20 October 2026 — if you are near your deadline, act on the rule that exists today. Source: alberta.ca/proclamations (OIC 339/2026).

What this covers

Complaints that fit

  • Job-placement or salary claims that turned out to be false
  • Accreditation or certification that does not exist
  • Refunds refused or never paid
  • Programs cancelled or materially changed
  • Undisclosed or improper fees
  • Terms of the enrolment contract broken

Goes somewhere else

  • Grade appeals — the college's academic process
  • Discrimination or harassment — provincial human rights commission
  • Public colleges and universities — their own ombudsperson
  • Anything needing legal advice — a lawyer, and we will say so plainly
